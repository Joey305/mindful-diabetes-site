# Verified scientific and editorial sources

Research reviewed: October 13, 2026. Prefer the linked primary papers and professional guidance to media summaries. Read the original source before modifying numerical claims.

## 1. Lead study: 2026 systemic proteomics

- Kurgan N, Kjaergaard J, Jespersen NZ, et al. *Systemic proteomic dynamics of acute exercise reveal genetic links to health and disease.* Nature Metabolism. Published October 12, 2026.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s42255-026-01621-w
- Journal: https://www.nature.com/articles/s42255-026-01621-w
- Study design: 25 healthy young men, one 45-minute moderate-intensity cycling bout, around 75% HRmax.
- Sampling: plasma and saliva before exercise and at approximately 0, 0.5, 1, 3, and 24 hours after; urine before exercise and approximately 0 and 24 hours afterward.
- Measurement: 10,128 proteins detected across fluids; 3,007 significantly regulated immediately after exercise. Important: these are measured proteomic changes, NOT 3,007 proven exerkines. Significance threshold reported as adjusted P < 0.05.
- Time-effect analysis identified 2,156 plasma, 601 saliva, and 272 urine proteins affected over time using the paper's stated criteria. Do not conflate time-effect counts with the 3,007 proteins changed at time 0, or sum across fluids as if every protein were unique.
- Distinct temporal dynamics across fluids; replication of many plasma responses in a separate cohort; human genetic linkage analysis; selected candidate functional validation in Drosophila.
- Limitations for popular article: small homogeneous discovery cohort, single acute bout, correlations do not independently prove messaging between named organs, genetic associations do not establish direct clinical benefit.

## 2. Short-bout exercise trial: 2026 glucose control

- Babir FJ, Marcotte-Chenard A, Sandilands RE, et al. *Exercise snacks performed in real-world settings reduce postprandial hyperglycaemia and glycaemic variability in individuals living with type 2 diabetes: a randomised crossover study.* Diabetologia 69, 2200-2211 (2026). Published April 24, 2026.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1007/s00125-026-06741-2
- Journal: https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00125-026-06741-2
- Study design: n=31, inactive, well-controlled type 2 diabetes, not insulin treated; two 48-hour real-world conditions under standardized diet, randomized crossover; four 1-minute VIGOROUS bodyweight bouts per day during the exercise condition.
- Findings: small statistically significant improvements in measures of glycemic variability and postprandial hyperglycemia; average glucose reduction did NOT meet significance (p=0.07); no significant difference for every CGM endpoint (for instance broad time-in-range).
- Limitations: 2-day intervention; small group; non-insulin-treated type 2 diabetes only; long-term outcomes untested. Never say 4 minutes of walking equals the study's vigorous protocol. Never call this proof of equivalent effects to 45 minutes of cycling.

## 3. Established GLUT4 mechanism

- Richter EA, Bilan PJ, Klip A. *A comprehensive view of muscle glucose uptake: regulation by insulin, contractile activity, and exercise.* Physiological Reviews. 2025;105(3).
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1152/physrev.00033.2024
- GLUT4 translocation to muscle cell surface responds to both insulin and contraction through partly distinct upstream pathways. This simplified physiology is established and distinct from proof of a specific 2026 exerkine pathway.

## 4. Exerkines conceptual review

- Chow LS, Gerszten RE, Taylor JM, et al. *Exerkines in health, resilience and disease.* Nature Reviews Endocrinology. 2022;18:273-289.
- DOI: https://doi.org/10.1038/s41574-022-00641-2
- Exerkines are released in response to acute/chronic exercise and can include signaling moieties from different tissues. Myokines come from muscle. Molecules that change abundance after exercise are not automatically exerkines.

## 5. Current professional guidance

- ADA 2026 Standards of Care, section 5: https://doi.org/10.2337/dc26-S005
- Break prolonged sitting at least every 30 minutes for people with diabetes, where possible; individualize and gradually progress activity. Most adults with diabetes: aim toward 150 minutes or more of moderate-to-vigorous aerobic exercise weekly.
- WHO physical activity and sedentary behaviour guidelines: https://www.who.int/publications-detail-redirect/9789240015128
- For adults generally, 150-300 minutes moderate or 75-150 vigorous activity weekly (or equivalent), plus strengthening activities on at least two days weekly. Any amount is better than none.
